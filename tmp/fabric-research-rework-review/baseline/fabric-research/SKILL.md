# Research

Optimize time to a well-supported answer. Main owns the synthesis.

## Browser rule

Use search and content-retrieval tools, not browser automation or browser-backed recovery. Set `web_search`'s `workflow: "none"`; do not opt into browser-cookie fetching with `fetch_content.auth`. Use an accessible alternative or report the source gap. Carry this rule into every worker assignment.

## Shape the work to the question

Cover the user's actual questions, definitions, source needs and requested output. Keep relevant dates and versions in the task and source evidence. Use a checklist or comparison table when it prevents omissions, not for every lookup.

For substantive comparisons, disputed questions, or consequential decisions, establish a compact research contract before retrieval or dispatch: decision and intended use; consequence of error; scope, exclusions, definitions and dates; decision criteria and evidence needed for each; variables that must remain comparable; requested output slots; and sufficient-evidence, gap or blocked-conclusion conditions. Keep it internal unless sharing prevents misunderstanding. Ask one grouped clarification only when an unresolved answer changes the evidence standard, work architecture or decision; otherwise state the narrowest reasonable assumption and proceed. User criteria and output slots may be fixed upfront; empirical categories, rankings and recommendations must follow evidence.

Start with direct retrieval when Main can efficiently resolve the question, even if it needs several sources. Delegate distinct uncertainties when parallel work or a different method earns its assignment and reconciliation cost. Before substantive fan-out, read the [stream contracts](references/stream-contracts.md) and map each assignment to an unresolved criterion or output slot. For narrow delegated checks, give the question, scope, context, known sources and Browser rule, and request supporting URLs/passages, contradictions and gaps without a full template. Workers supply evidence, not the final cross-question recommendation.

Distinguish parallel execution, independent analysis, independent evidence and dependent verification. When independent analysis is required, workers form findings without sharing intermediate conclusions; overlapping subjects need distinct methods, evidence classes or adversarial hypotheses. Dependent checks may consume earlier findings but are not independent discovery streams. One worker can inspect independent sources without supplying multiple independent worker judgments.

Honor the user's exact counting unit: an exact agent count includes verification and repair launches; an exact research-stream count constrains the named research assignments and is not automatically an all-agent count. Record unique assignments and launches in a dispatch ledger. Do not silently add, merge, replace or remove fixed assignments. Clarify only materially ambiguous counts; Main can verify directly when another launch would exceed an agent limit.

Use as many useful workers as the task needs, accounting for native availability, provider throttling and explicit user budgets; reserve capacity for verification and synthesis. Parallelize independent retrieval and analysis; start dependent checks when their inputs arrive, without waiting for unrelated workers. Avoid repeated searches and transferring entire reports where a focused finding will do.

Configure every worker launched by this skill, including verification and last30days workers, with `runner: "pi"`, `model: "openai-codex/gpt-5.6-terra"`, `thinking: "high"`, and `extensions: true`. Pass these options explicitly to `agent` / `workflow.agent`, `agents.run`, and `agents.spawn` rather than inheriting model or thinking defaults. Before dispatch, confirm the exact model key in `tools.models()`; if unavailable, report the blocker rather than silently substituting a model. This policy does not change Main's model or global defaults.

Have each worker first verify its required actions through effective discovery; Main's tool access does not prove child access. In each Pi worker request, grant every required captured action by its registered tool name in `tools`, for example `tools: ["web_search", "fetch_content", "get_search_content", "source_check"]`. `extensions: true` enables the Fabric execution surface; `tools: ["fabric_exec"]` alone does not grant captured actions. Inside `fabric_exec`, workers call granted captured actions as `extensions.<name>(...)`, never as top-level functions. Give workers only the tools their assignment needs, but do not treat an allowlist or prompt as proof of isolation. Prohibit unrelated mutations and further delegation unless explicitly assigned. Permit only necessary task-scoped artifacts, such as the last30days plan and output; do not authorize installs, configuration changes, or publishing.

In full-code mode, use `fabric_exec` for all execution, including direct retrieval: `pi.*` for core tools, `extensions.*` for confirmed captured actions, and first-class provider proxies for known actions. Reserve `tools.call({ ref, args })` for discovered or computed refs. Keep intermediate work in code and return compact evidence, decisions and failures.

For finite fan-out, use `agent` with `parallel` or `pipeline`. Pass thunks to `parallel`, not already-started promises; keep dependent stages ordered. Use `agents.spawn` when Main benefits from consuming or redirecting work between turns. Consume terminal notifications when enabled, or use `agents.wait` when the program needs the result; do not poll for completion. Labels and progress should help follow real work. Use a schema only when machine aggregation benefits from it. Workflow helpers return the schema value or final text; use `agents.run` when native status and usage envelopes are needed. Native budgets and results remain authoritative. Keep Pi and child state in `/home/balauru/.pi-profiles/fabric` via `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`; `cwd` does not select a profile.

## Find and check evidence

Use the existing `web_search`, `fetch_content`, `get_search_content`, and `source_check` tools with their configured providers; omit provider overrides. Before first use, discover the actions needed for this task with bounded `tools.search` or `tools.list`, then inspect their effective contracts with `tools.describe`. A tool visible outside Fabric is not necessarily registered as `extensions.<name>`. Discovery establishes availability, not working credentials or successful retrieval. If a required action is missing, use an available alternative that preserves the task and Browser rule, or report the capability gap; do not install tools or change configuration to repair it.

For substantive research, prefer `web_search.queries` with 2–4 distinct angles rather than synonymous repetitions; keep a single query for narrow lookups. Put query text and other awkward content in named `payloads` and use the matching `π` keys.

Check captured results' `isError` before treating them as evidence, and inspect tool-specific failure fields according to the discovered contract. Keep full retrieved results inside the program when useful; return only relevant claims, supporting URLs and passages or locators, contradictions, gaps, and retrieval handles such as `responseId` for later expansion. Use `get_search_content` with `findText` to locate support in stored content instead of repeatedly returning whole reports. Preserve decisive passages and table context when reducing output.

Prefer original, accessible sources suited to the claim: primary documentation for specifications, original papers for research, reproducible independent measurements for performance, and direct community posts for reported experience. Treat summaries and search answers as leads. Add recent-field research only when practitioner experience, regressions, sentiment or changing conditions could materially change the answer, not merely because a skill is installed. For recent-discussion research, read the [last30days integration](references/last30days.md); it remains the authority for execution and structured coverage checks, not badges or footers.

Check decisive and disputed claims against the supporting passage or table. Reuse already retrieved evidence when it contains that support; retrieve a lead when it does not. Missing administrative metadata does not invalidate useful evidence. A missing supporting source does limit what can be concluded.

Trace shared origins: syndicated stories, repeated benchmarks and separate workers citing one paper are not independent corroboration. Resolve material contradictions by inspecting methods, populations, definitions and source authority; keep genuine disagreement visible. Compare numbers only when tasks, versions, settings, samples, metrics and denominators support the comparison. Do not turn unsupported vendor claims or anecdotes into controlled measurements, or average incompatible results. For quantitative rankings, causal claims or literature transfer, apply the relevant methodology gates in [synthesis and reporting](references/synthesis-and-reporting.md), even when Main researched directly.

For high-consequence decisions or materially disputed recommendations, name the evidence that would overturn or narrow the leading conclusion and perform a bounded counterevidence check. Seek relevant null results, regressions, failure conditions, bias and omitted costs. Main or an existing assignment can own it; another worker is not mandatory. Report the strongest counterevidence or the bounded search gap, not an invented absence of opposition.

## Close the evidence gap, then answer

For complex or disputed synthesis, including work done directly by Main, read [synthesis and reporting](references/synthesis-and-reporting.md). Reconcile one material-claim ledger before prose, reusing worker evidence rows rather than creating parallel ledgers. It owns claim-status definitions, confidence, and evidence dispositions; do not force a ledger onto a simple lookup or into every final answer.

Validate execution status separately from evidence usability. For substantive returns, check the applicable stream-contract fields against actual evidence, not just the presence of fields. A terminal success can leave its conclusion unsupported; a failed worker can leave usable partial evidence. Accept supported portions, mark missing requirements and recover only decision-changing gaps.

For delegated batches, account for requested, dispatched and completed items with named gaps, and return `success`, `partial` or `failed` according to usable evidence and coverage. Catch ordinary failures per independent item so one rejection does not discard successful sibling results. Inspect native worker status/error fields when available; receiving text alone does not establish successful completion. A worker that did not successfully discover and invoke a required retrieval action has supplied no source-backed external evidence, even if it returns plausible URLs or passages or says retrieval succeeded; mark the gap or retrieve independently. Preserve successful work when a tool or worker fails. Inspect the actual failure and recover only the evidence still needed; do not rerun successful siblings or restart a whole batch solely because it is partial. Retry, change sources, delegate or stop according to the remaining gap and the likely value of more work within the user's budget. Stop when support is sufficient or further retrieval is unlikely to change the answer; report material unresolved gaps rather than manufacturing completeness.

Redirect a still-live worker when that preserves useful context. Do not assume a completed Fabric one-shot can be resumed or steered. For terminal gaps, Main may retrieve directly or launch a narrowly scoped repair only when the user's count and budget permit it. Do not impose a universal retry count. Account for all planned work as terminal or explicitly incomplete before the final answer; this does not prevent incremental synthesis or dependent checks.

Main synthesizes with citations rather than concatenating worker reports. Every material external claim in the final answer must resolve to a direct supporting URL, inline or through a clearly mapped source identifier; an internal ledger alone is not a final citation. Separate sourced findings from inference and recommendation, using explicit evidence status where ambiguity matters. The user's requested structure wins; otherwise use the smallest focused, comparative or decision-grade form in the synthesis reference. Keep recommendations within the evidence, name their applicability and important trade-offs, and explain decision-changing disagreements and limitations. For consequential recommendations, include failure or escalation conditions and the evidence that would change the action. When a choice remains unresolved, propose the smallest gap-specific observation or evaluation, with fixed variables, an appropriate validator and a decision-changing result condition. Do not invent universal sample sizes or thresholds.

Complete when every required criterion and output slot has support, an explicit gap or a blocked conclusion; planned work is accounted for; material claims have checked support and appropriate status; quantitative comparisons and consequential counterevidence checks are satisfied or visibly limited; and recommendations stay within that coverage. Keep unknown cells unknown. For substantive work, record why retrieval stopped and the highest-impact remaining gap. Stop reasons must reflect the contract, not just convenient search results. A focused lookup needs only its answer, decisive support and material limitations, not a planning report or mandatory fan-out.
