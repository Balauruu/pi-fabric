# T2 Evidence Note: fixed-model coding-agent run design

**Scope and status:** **Partial, decision-grade for validation, context/tool feedback, and budget controls.** Sources were inspected as live original papers, dated through **2026-09-07**. “Fixed model” means the executor model, decoding settings, and reasoning effort remain unchanged within an arm. Results are not a universal ranking and do not establish security properties.

## Findings

### Material evidence units

| Unit | Decision dimension | Original source and located support | Method and comparator | Exact outcome and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Context selection and search-tool design | [SWE-agent, Fig. 5 and §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793): “**15.7%** … for No search vs. **12.0%** … with Iterative search.” | SWE-bench Lite, GPT-4 Turbo fixed; ACI variants. Iterative search exposed results one at a time, while the agent often exhaustively inspected matches. | Iterative search was **3.7 percentage points lower** than no search. The paper attributes this to context/cost exhaustion. This supports measuring information presentation, not merely tool availability. Source-unique here. |
| E2 | Retry and budget limits | [SWE-agent, Table 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793): six GPT-4 Turbo runs on SWE-bench Lite yielded **17.33%, 18.00%, 18.00%, 18.67%, 17.33%, 18.33%**, mean **17.94 ± 0.49 percentage points**. | Same agent/model, six independent runs. Per-instance inference cap was **US$4**. | Aggregate variance was modest but per-instance outcomes changed. Pass@1 was 17.94%; pass@6 was 32.67%, so retries alter outcome and cost exposure. Do not use one rollout to choose a loop. Source-unique here. |
| E3 | Validation quality and repair-loop feedback | [Validation Evidence, §§3–5, Tables 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28871): “**46.0%** of positive comparable events carry no bug-discriminating information”; **23.8%** of baseline rollouts had evidence-inadequate closure. | gpt-5.6-sol repair agent, 110 tasks split equally between SWE-bench Verified and SWE-rebench; 643 rollouts and 3,730 retained post-edit validation events. BSG-VA replayed each validation command on buggy (B), candidate (S), and gold-fix (G) states. | Of 2,548 positive comparable events, 1,141 were regression-only and 30 misleading. A passing test alone is not adequate closure evidence. Source-unique here. |
| E4 | Validation feedback intervention | [Validation Evidence, §5.4 and Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28871): BCF versus attention-matched reminder reduced evidence-inadequate closure by **7.8 pp** (95% CI **−12.9 to −2.7 pp**, **p=0.0029**) and increased bug-discriminating evidence by **7.4 pp** (95% CI **+1.7 to +13.0 pp**, **p=0.011**). | Prespecified three-arm, task-level paired experiment. Same executor, task, and loop; intervention returned B-replay outcome after eligible positive validation. Median B-replay overhead: **11.0 seconds/rollout**. | Repair success changed by **+0.5 pp**, not detectably. The effect is on validation evidence, not established issue-resolution improvement. Estimate was below the prespecified **10-pp SESOI**. Source-unique here. |
| E5 | Decomposition versus unconstrained tool loop | [Agentless, abstract and §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489): structured localization → repair → validation attained **32.00%** (96 fixes) on SWE-bench Lite at **US$0.70**. | Hierarchical file/element/edit-location selection, sampled Search/Replace patches, generated reproduction tests, then patch ranking. | It is actionable evidence that explicit stages can be economical. It is **not** a clean fixed-model causal contrast with a matched interactive agent, so it must not be used to claim decomposition caused the result. Source-unique here. |
| E6 | Triggered rather than periodic intervention | [LivePlan, Tables II–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06701v1): up to **+15.24 pp** resolution, mean **+9.9 pp**, reported additional cost **US$0.08/instance**; advisor cost **US$0.01–0.06/instance**. | SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified and SWE-bench Pro-Python. Deterministic monitor triggered advisor feedback, compared with vanilla, periodic advisor, predefined advice, and re-planning. Fixed executor within each comparison, but advisor is an additional model. | Periodic advice triggered more often and performed worse, reportedly due to misleading advice. This is evidence for event-triggered control, but **not** a pure single-model run-design result. Source-unique here. |

### Operational interpretation

| Run-design choice | Evidence-supported action | Adoption rule |
|---|---|---|
| Context/search | Present compact, ranked, bounded evidence. Log result count, displayed tokens, and repeated inspection. | Reject a search interface if paired resolve rate falls or it materially raises context/tool use without a predeclared compensating benefit. E1 shows “more search” can be worse. |
| Tool feedback | Return actionable, task-specific observations rather than generic success/failure. | Keep feedback only when it increases a measured endpoint under matched budgets. Do not attribute gains to JSON or formatting alone without a content-matched control. |
| Validation | Require at least one bug-discriminating validation event when feasible: fail on B, pass on S, and preferably pass on G/reference behavior. | Do not auto-promote a patch solely because its test passed on S. Record regression-only, candidate-specific, misleading, and unevaluable events. |
| Repair/retries | Use bounded retries with fixed total cost, token, wall-clock, and tool-call ceilings. | Evaluate pass@1 and the complete retry curve. A rerun policy is acceptable only if marginal resolved tasks and evidence quality justify marginal cost. |
| Decomposition | Trial an explicit localize → patch → validate pipeline against the current adaptive loop. | Treat decomposition as a hypothesis, not a default. Keep its candidate count and validation budget equal to baseline. |
| Steering | Trigger repair guidance from deterministic observable conditions, such as repeated failed edits, stale phase progress, or missing validation. | Prefer an event-triggered rule over periodic advice only after a matched test. If an advisor model is added, classify it as a model-stack change, not fixed-model optimization. |

## Concrete paired evaluation artifact

| Field | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Population | Stratified production-like holdout: repository, language, issue type, reference-patch size, and historical difficulty. Exclude tasks available before the model’s cutoff where possible. Freeze task images, evaluator commit, and harness commit. |
| Pairing | For each task \(i\), run baseline **A** and one design change **B** with the **same model snapshot, reasoning effort, system prompt except intervention, temperature/seed policy, repository state, tool permissions, and total budgets**. Randomize A/B order. |
| Primary endpoint | Resolved by hidden/reference tests, reported as paired difference and 95% paired-bootstrap CI. |
| Required secondary endpoints | Cost per attempted task and per resolved task, wall time, input/output tokens, tool calls, retries, malformed calls, patch size, and BSG-VA validation-evidence role. |
| Validation endpoint | Evidence-inadequate closure rate, plus rate of gold-aligned bug-discriminating checks. A patch with only S-pass evidence is not “validated.” |
| Stop/adopt rule | Pre-register a smallest effect size of interest. Adopt only if the lower CI bound for resolution or validation-quality benefit exceeds the operational threshold **and** the upper CI bound for cost/latency is within the allowed increase. Otherwise retain baseline or mark inconclusive. |
| Minimum comparison set | A: current loop. B1: compact/ranked context. B2: validation-evidence feedback. B3: bounded retry policy. B4: staged decomposition. Test one change per arm first. |
| Retained per-run artifact | `task_id, repository_sha, harness_sha, model_snapshot, reasoning_effort, seed, arm, prompt_hash, tool_policy_hash, budgets, trajectory, observations, patch, tests invoked, B/S/G outcomes, evaluator result, tokens, cost, latency, termination reason`. |

## Counterevidence and limitations

1. **Tool additions can regress performance.** E1 directly contradicts the assumption that richer navigation is beneficial. The failure mode was exhaustive inspection, which consumed context/cost budget.

2. **Passing validation is not equivalent to fixing the reported defect.** E3 found 46.0% of comparable positive validation events were regression-only or misleading. E4 improved evidence quality but did **not** detectably improve repair success.

3. **Run variance is decision-relevant.** E2’s low aggregate standard deviation does not make individual-task outcomes stable. Paired multi-run comparisons are needed, especially for retry or termination changes.

4. **Cross-paper comparisons are not a leaderboard.** SWE-agent, Agentless, Validation Evidence, and LivePlan differ in model, benchmark, agent scaffold, budget, date, and endpoint. E5 and E6 provide design hypotheses, not causal evidence for a fixed single-model production agent.

5. **Benchmark and evaluator transfer risk remains.** SWE-bench-style tasks are public historical GitHub issues. They can have contamination or memorization exposure, and hidden-test resolution measures compatibility with that evaluator rather than all production correctness. Use temporally fresh/private holdouts and report the evaluator version and test oracle.

6. **Validation replay has coverage limits.** BSG-VA requires replayable commands and B/S/G states. It does not prove behavior outside exercised tests, nor does it assess security.
