## Material evidence units

### M1 — Game of 24: large gain, high inference cost

- **Source:** Yao et al., *Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models*, [original HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601), §4.1 and Appendix B.3, Tables 7.
- **Claim/result:** On 100 relatively hard Game-of-24 instances, GPT-4 ToT with breadth \(b=5\) scored **74%** success. The paper reports **IO 7.3%**, **CoT 4.0%**, **CoT self-consistency 9.0%**, and **best-of-100 CoT 49%**. The exact cost-table rows inspected are:
  - **CoT best-of-100:** **6.7k / 2.2k completion/prompt tokens**, **$0.47**, **49%**.
  - **ToT:** **5.5k / 1.4k completion/prompt tokens**, **$0.74**, **74%**.
- **Method/comparator:** Chat-completions GPT-4, temperature 0.7, experiments May 5–16 2023. ToT decomposes each puzzle into three intermediate equations, uses proposal prompting, GPT-4 value judgments sampled three times per candidate, and BFS retaining five states. CoT had three intermediate equations. Baselines were sampled 100 times per puzzle.
- **Direct support:** “ToT requires 5.5k completion tokens, close to 100 CoT trials (6.7k tokens). But the performance of ToT is better than best of 100 independent CoT trials” (Appendix B.3). The §4.1 results paragraph gives the success rates and task construction.
- **Decision dimension:** correctness versus per-request completion, prompt-token, and dollar cost.
- **Source-unique:** Yes.

### M2 — Creative writing: modest quality gain versus CoT, but refinement exceeds ToT

- **Source:** [original HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601), §4.2 and Appendix B.3, Table 8.
- **Claim/result:** On 100 generated four-paragraph constrained-writing inputs, GPT-4-rated coherence was **ToT 7.56**, **IO 6.19**, and **CoT 6.93** on a 1–10 scale. Blind author judgments preferred ToT over CoT in **41/100** pairs, CoT in **21/100**, and found **38/100** similarly coherent. Iterative refinement raised IO to **7.67** and ToT to **7.91**.
- **Method/comparator:** GPT-4, temperature 0.7. ToT generated five plans, voted five times, retained one plan, generated five passages, then voted five times. Automatic ratings used five GPT-4 samples per output. Human evaluation was a blinded, randomized-order comparison by a subset of authors.
- **Cost:** Table 8’s inspected ToT row is **4k / 2.9k completion/prompt tokens** and **$0.32** per task. The paper characterizes this as roughly **5×** the completion tokens and money cost of its writing baseline. It estimates Game-of-24 plus Creative-Writing main ToT experiments as **$106**: \(0.74\times100 + 0.32\times100\).
- **Direct support:** §4.2 reports the ratings, preferences, and refinement results. Appendix B.3 states “ToT takes around 5x completion tokens and money cost.”
- **Decision dimension:** quality, evaluator reliability, and cost.
- **Source-unique:** Yes.

### M3 — Transfer is task- and model-dependent

- **Source:** [original HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601), Appendix B.1–B.3.
- **Claim/result:** GPT-3.5 ToT reached **19%** on Game of 24, versus GPT-4 ToT’s **74%**. Hybrid runs were **64%** for GPT-4 generation plus GPT-3.5 evaluation and **31%** for GPT-3.5 generation plus GPT-4 evaluation. The authors infer that thought generation, not evaluation, was the bottleneck in that setup.
- **Method/comparator:** Game-of-24 ToT, with the GPT-3.5 condition changing from a one-shot to a three-shot proposal prompt.
- **Direct support:** Appendix B.2: “GPT-3.5+ToT’s 19% is far worse than GPT-4+ToT’s 74%,” followed by the hybrid results.
- **Decision dimension:** model portability and cost-reduction strategy.
- **Source-unique:** Yes.

## Counterevidence and limitations

1. **Reproduction regression:** The official repository says its later reproduction of the reported GPT-4 Game-of-24 ToT configuration achieved **69%**, not the paper’s **74%**, “due to randomness in GPT decoding.” This makes a single-run point estimate unsuitable as a production expectation. [Official repository](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm).
2. **Not a universal reasoning benchmark:** The three main tasks were constructed specifically to challenge GPT-4 with planning/search. The paper itself says common NLP tasks may be too easy for GPT-4 and may not require ToT.
3. **Evaluator dependence:** Game-of-24 success has executable validity checks. Writing quality uses GPT-4 scoring and a small author-only blind study. The latter is useful corroboration, but neither establishes quality transfer to a production text domain.
4. **Cost is incomplete:** The paper reports completion/prompt tokens and historical dollar amounts, not latency, queueing, retries, tool costs, or current provider pricing. Do not translate its 2023 dollar values into current production cost.
5. **CoT/self-consistency boundary:** Their Game-of-24 comparator results are valid only as reported by the ToT paper. Their broader effects and costs were not independently inspected here.

## Practical selection and local evaluation

| Signal | Adopt / test | Avoid or stop |
|---|---|---|
| Problem has discrete intermediate states, verifiable constraints, and valuable backtracking | Pair a bounded ToT search with a baseline | Do not use unconstrained tree search |
| Single-pass CoT fails early or misses recoverable branches | Test small beam breadth and evaluator repetitions | Stop if search fails to improve success per dollar |
| A reliable external validator or high-quality state evaluator exists | Measure candidate-generation and evaluator errors separately | Do not trust self-evaluation alone for high-stakes output |
| Quality is subjective text | Compare against iterative refinement and blinded human/domain rubrics | Do not rely only on LLM-as-judge scores |

**Minimal paired local evaluation artifact**

| Arm | Fixed variables | Report |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Same model, prompt context, temperature, token cap, tool access | Task success, latency, completion/prompt tokens, provider cost |
| CoT | Same variables, explicit intermediate reasoning | Same measures |
| Bounded ToT | Pre-register thought unit, beam width, generation count, evaluator count, pruning rule, maximum nodes | Same measures plus nodes visited, prune rate, evaluator agreement, valid-final-output rate |
| Refinement, when text quality is the endpoint | Same model and maximum total token budget | Same measures plus blind domain-rater preference |

Use paired inputs, deterministic validators where possible, and report distributions and repeated runs rather than one success rate. A stop condition is warranted if ToT’s incremental validated-success gain does not justify its incremental total cost and latency.
