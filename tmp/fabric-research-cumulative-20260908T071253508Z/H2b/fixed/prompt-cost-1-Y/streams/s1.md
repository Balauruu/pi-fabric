# Tree of Thoughts: original evidence on quality, cost, and adoption

## Assigned question and boundary

**Question:** Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct input-output (IO) or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, and what does the original study’s quality/cost evidence support?

**Bounded answer:** **Do not adopt ToT as a default production workflow on this paper alone.** The paper supports a conditional trial where tasks have short, decomposable search states, meaningful branching/backtracking, and a verifier or sufficiently useful state evaluator, and where CoT demonstrably fails. It does not establish transfer to ordinary production tasks, modern models, or cases where self-evaluation is unreliable.

Scope is limited to the frozen original-paper materials for arXiv v2, including the cost-table extraction. No outside studies or universal ranking claims.

Primary source: [Tree of Thoughts, arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2)

## Measured findings

### Game of 24

**Task and metric.** 100 relatively hard games, indices 901–1,000 from a 1,362-game source. A success must use each of four inputs exactly once in a valid equation equaling 24. Metric is success rate across the 100 games. ([§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1))

**Common conditions.** Chat-completion GPT-4, temperature 0.7, experiments May 5–16, 2023. ([§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4))

| Condition | Measured result | Comparator qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| IO, 5-shot | 7.3% | 100 samples per game, average performance |
| CoT | 4.0% | CoT demonstrations included three intermediate equations |
| CoT self-consistency | 9.0% | Majority output from 100 CoT samples |
| IO, oracle best-of-100 | 33% | Oracle selection, not deployable selection |
| CoT, oracle best-of-100 | 49% | Oracle selection, not deployable selection |
| ToT BFS, breadth 1 | 45% | Search/evaluation rather than independent full-chain sampling |
| ToT BFS, breadth 5 | 74% | Main ToT condition |

Sources: [§4.1 Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1), [Appendix B.3 / Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3).

**ToT condition.** Three thought steps, each an intermediate equation. A sequential “propose” prompt generated next-step candidates. BFS retained the best five states per step. GPT-4 classified candidates as `sure/maybe/impossible`; values were sampled three times per thought. The paper attributes the gain to local alternatives plus heuristic pruning. It reports that about 60% of CoT samples had already failed after the first step. ([§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1))

**Cost per case.**

| Condition | Generate / prompt tokens | Cost per case | Success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IO, best-of-100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| CoT, best-of-100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |

Source: [Appendix B.3, Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T7).

**Interpretation limited to this condition.** ToT achieved 74% versus oracle best-of-100 CoT’s 49%, with fewer generated tokens (5.5k versus 6.7k) but a higher reported dollar cost ($0.74 versus $0.47). The original source labels the token column “Generate/Prompt tokens,” while surrounding prose calls the 5.5k figure *completion tokens*. The slash convention is not defined further, so these should not be recomputed or normalized beyond the published values.

### Creative Writing

**Task and metrics.** 100 inputs of four random sentences. The output must be a coherent four-paragraph passage ending each paragraph with the corresponding input sentence. There is no ground-truth passage. Coherence was assessed by (1) five GPT-4 zero-shot 1–10 scores averaged per output and (2) a blinded comparison by a subset of the authors between CoT and ToT outputs. ([§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2))

**Conditions and results.**

| Condition | Measured result |
|---|---:|
| IO, zero-shot | GPT-4 coherence score 6.19 |
| CoT, zero-shot plan then write | 6.93 |
| ToT | 7.56 |
| Iterative refinement on IO | 7.67 |
| Iterative refinement on ToT | 7.91 |
| Blind author comparison, ToT vs CoT | ToT preferred 41/100, CoT 21/100, tied 38/100 |

Source: [§4.2 Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2).

**ToT condition.** Depth two with one intermediate planning step. GPT-4 sampled five plans, sampled five votes to select one, then sampled five passages from that plan and selected one with five votes. Breadth was one after each vote. IO and CoT each produced 10 samples per task. ([§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2))

**Cost per case.**

| Condition | Generate / prompt tokens | Cost per case |
|---|---:|---:|
| IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 |
| CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 |
| ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 |

Source: [Appendix B.3, Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T8).

The paper describes ToT as approximately **5×** the completion-token and monetary cost of IO/CoT for this task. The published Table 8 caption incorrectly says “Cost analysis on Game of 24,” while its row header says “Creative Writing”; this source inconsistency should be retained rather than corrected silently.

### Experiment-level cost

The authors estimate main ToT experiments at:

- Game of 24: $0.74 × 100 cases
- Creative Writing: $0.32 × 100 cases
- **Total: about $106**
- Crosswords DFS: stated only as “within $100,” not an exact cost

They generalize only that ToT cost is prompt/search dependent and **could require 5–100× more generated tokens than CoT**. ([Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3))

## What the measured evidence supports

1. **Mechanism demonstrated in narrow cases.** On a hard, finite, externally verifiable arithmetic search task, ToT’s branch generation plus heuristic state evaluation substantially outperformed direct sampling, CoT, and even an oracle best-of-100 CoT comparator in the reported GPT-4 setup.

2. **Creative-writing result is quality evidence, not objective correctness evidence.** ToT exceeded IO and CoT on the paper’s GPT-4-based coherence score and its small author-judged pairwise comparison, but iterative refinement exceeded ToT when applied to IO (7.67 versus 7.56). Therefore this result does not establish ToT as the dominant general method.

3. **Cost is a real operational tradeoff, not a free quality gain.** Game of 24’s ToT used a generation volume near 100 CoT trials, was more successful than oracle best-of-100 CoT, and still cost more per case. Creative Writing cost roughly five times IO/CoT. The paper explicitly says ToT uses substantially more computation.

## Counterevidence and transfer limitations

- **Not a broad task sample.** The main study covers three deliberately constructed hard tasks and explicitly says ToT may be unnecessary for tasks GPT-4 already handles well. ([§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6))
- **Model and time dependence.** Results use an early GPT-4 chat-completion setup at temperature 0.7 in May 2023. They do not establish the same gain on a production model or current inference stack.
- **Task-specific prompting and algorithms.** Game of 24 used constrained sequential proposals, three-step BFS, and categorical valuation. Creative Writing used sample-and-vote twice. These are not one generic production configuration.
- **Evaluator error can destroy search.** In the crossword experiment, the model sometimes pruned actually solvable states because it judged unfamiliar or obsolete terms impossible. The paper says better DFS-pruning heuristics are critical. ([§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS3))
- **Creative quality measurement is entangled with the evaluated model.** GPT-4 scored GPT-4 outputs, and human assessment came from a subset of authors. The paper itself calls the automatic metric potentially noisy.
- **Oracle baselines are not production comparators.** Best-of-100 IO/CoT measures whether a successful sample existed, not an implementable selection policy. It is useful for compute/search comparison but cannot be deployed without a valid selector.
- **GPT-3.5 transfer was materially weaker on Game of 24.** GPT-3.5 ToT reached 19% versus GPT-4 ToT’s 74%, after changing the proposal prompt from one-shot to three-shot. Mixed generation/evaluation conditions were 64% for GPT-4 generation plus GPT-3.5 evaluation and 31% in the reverse direction. ([Appendix B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2))

## Proposed adoption boundary

**Recommendation:** use direct or CoT prompting as the default. Consider ToT only for a workflow slice meeting all of these proposed controls:

- The task has a small, explicit sequence of intermediate states.
- Multiple next actions are genuinely plausible and early choices cause irreversible or expensive downstream failure.
- A reliable local verifier, outcome signal, or empirically validated state evaluator can rank/prune candidates.
- CoT fails materially at an acceptable baseline budget.
- The quality increase is worth measured token, dollar, latency, and operational-complexity increases.

Do **not** treat hoped-for improvements in open-ended, knowledge-limited, or evaluator-unreliable cases as a ToT adoption gate. Those are exploratory cases, not demonstrated mechanism cases.

## Proposed paired local evaluation

### A. Demonstrated-mechanism test

Use a held-out, production-representative set of **verifiable multi-step search tasks**, stratified by difficulty and failure mode.

For every case, run the same production model and prompt context under:

1. direct IO
2. CoT
3. CoT with a production-feasible selector, if applicable
4. ToT with explicit thought granularity, branching factor, evaluator, pruning rule, depth limit, and stop rule

Evaluate paired outcomes using:

- verified task success
- input, output, and total tokens
- API cost
- wall-clock latency and tail latency
- number of model calls, branches, prunes, and verifier/evaluator errors
- failure stage, especially whether ToT recovers from an early bad state

Run two budget views:

- **Fixed-cost:** cap all arms at the same dollar/token budget.
- **Fixed-quality-target:** estimate the least cost and latency to reach a preset acceptable success rate.

Adopt only if ToT’s paired improvement is operationally meaningful and remains positive after its full cost and latency are counted.

### B. Exploratory transfer test

Separately test open-ended or knowledge-dependent workflows, but label this exploratory. Use blinded human evaluation with pre-registered rubrics and independent raters, plus safety and factuality checks. Compare ToT against both CoT and iterative refinement because the original Creative Writing result shows refinement can match or exceed ToT.
