# Tree of Thoughts adoption boundary: original-evidence note

## Assigned question and scope

Should production reasoning adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct input-output (IO) or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, based **only** on the original paper and supplied original-format cost extraction? This note covers Game of 24 and Creative Writing, their measured quality and token/cost conditions, and transfer limits. It does not rank techniques universally.

**Bounded answer:** **Do not adopt ToT as a default production workflow.** The study supports a targeted trial only for tasks where direct/CoT materially fail and the task has useful intermediate states that an LM can generate and evaluate. Its strongest Game of 24 result is large but task-specific. Creative Writing shows a smaller, evaluator-dependent gain that iterative refinement exceeds. ToT incurred substantially higher per-case cost.

## Measured findings

### F1. Game of 24: large improvement under a structured, hard 100-case benchmark

The task required an equation that equals 24 while using each of four inputs exactly once. Evaluation used 100 relatively hard games, indices 901–1,000 of 1,362 scraped games. All otherwise-unspecified experiments used Chat Completion GPT-4, temperature 0.7, conducted May 5–16, 2023. [§4, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4), [Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2).

| Condition | Measured success | Comparator details |
|---|---:|---|
| IO | 7.3% | 5-shot IO, averaged over 100 samples per game |
| CoT | 4.0% | 5-shot format augmented with three intermediate equations, averaged over 100 samples per game |
| CoT self-consistency | 9.0% | Majority output of 100 CoT samples |
| ToT, breadth 1 | 45% | Three equation steps |
| ToT, breadth 5 | 74% | BFS, retain best five candidates per step |

Source: [§4.1 Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1).

The 74% ToT configuration used three intermediate-equation thought steps, a common proposal prompt, BFS with \(b=5\), and three LM value samples per thought, classifying reachability as “sure/maybe/impossible.” The paper’s oracle comparison is **best-of-\(k\)** independent samples, not an ordinary deployed selection rule: best-of-100 CoT reached 49%, below ToT’s 74%. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1), [Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T7).

### F2. Game of 24 cost: ToT improved on best-of-100 CoT but cost more

| Condition | Generate / prompt tokens per case | Cost per case | Success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IO, best of 100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| CoT, best of 100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |

Source: [Appendix B.3, Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T7).

For 100 cases, the table implies $13, $47, and $74 respectively. Thus, ToT cost about **1.57×** best-of-100 CoT while using fewer first-column tokens, and delivered **+25 percentage points** success. It cost **5.7×** best-of-100 CoT’s per-case price relative to? Correction: $0.74/$0.47 = **1.57×**, not 5.7×.

**Qualification:** the table header says “Generate/Prompt tokens,” while the prose calls the 5.5k ToT figure “completion tokens.” The original source does not define the slash convention further, so the labels should be preserved rather than reinterpreted.

### F3. Creative Writing: quality gain, but not a decisive superiority result

The task used 100 inputs of four random sentences and required a coherent four-paragraph passage ending each paragraph with the respective sentence. There was no ground-truth passage. GPT-4 scored each output five times on a 1–10 coherence scale, then scores were averaged. A subset of authors also performed a blinded pairwise CoT-versus-ToT comparison. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2).

| Condition | Measured outcome | Conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| IO | GPT-4 coherence 6.19 | Zero-shot, 10 samples/task |
| CoT | 6.93 | Zero-shot brief-plan-then-write, 10 samples/task |
| ToT | 7.56 | Depth 2, five plans then five votes, retain one plan, then five passages and five votes |
| Human CoT vs ToT | ToT preferred 41, CoT 21, tie 38 of 100 | Blind author-subset pair comparison |
| Iterative refine from IO | 7.67 | Up to five iterations from one random IO sample |
| Iterative refine from ToT | 7.91 | Up to five iterations |

Source: [§4.2 Results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2).

Therefore, ToT exceeded direct and CoT under the paper’s GPT-4 coherence judge, but **iterative refinement from IO (7.67) exceeded ToT alone (7.56)**. This is direct counterevidence to a broad “use ToT rather than alternatives” conclusion.

### F4. Creative Writing cost: roughly fivefold

| Condition | Generate / prompt tokens per case | Cost per case |
|---|---:|---:|
| IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 |
| CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 |
| ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 |

Source: [Appendix B.3, Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T8).

For 100 cases, this implies $6 IO, $7 CoT, and $32 ToT: **5.33× IO cost** and **4.57× CoT cost**. The paper says the main Game of 24 plus Creative Writing ToT experiments cost about **$106** (\($0.74×100 + $0.32×100\)); it estimates Crosswords DFS was also within $100. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3).

**Source-format discrepancy:** Table 8’s published caption incorrectly says “Cost analysis on Game of 24,” but its row header says “Creative Writing.” The values above retain the table’s Creative Writing row header rather than silently correcting the caption.

## Limitations and transfer boundary

### F5. Evidence does not establish general production superiority

1. **Narrow task set and dated model condition.** The paper explicitly calls this an initial study of three relatively simple tasks designed to challenge GPT-4. Its main measurements are a May 2023 GPT-4 Chat Completion configuration, not a current-model or production-workflow comparison. [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4), [§6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6).

2. **Task-specific search design.** Game of 24 benefits from exact intermediate equations and a meaningful reachability classifier. Creative Writing uses plans, pairwise voting, a GPT-4 judge, and author-subset human ratings. Those structures do not automatically exist in a local workflow.

3. **Selection asymmetry.** “Best of 100” IO/CoT is explicitly an oracle calculation. It demonstrates opportunity in sampled paths, not a deployable output-selection policy unless the local task has an equivalent reliable verifier or selector.

4. **Imperfect self-evaluation.** The paper says values need only be approximately helpful, not correct. Its crossword ablation showed pruning could discard actual solutions. This exposes a general operational risk: a weak evaluator can convert additional search into confidently pruned good paths. [§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S3), [§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS3).

5. **Cost and latency scale with search.** The authors state ToT can require 5–100× more generated tokens than CoT, depending on prompts and search algorithm. The paper measures API cost, not end-to-end production latency, tail latency, reliability, or operational maintenance. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3).

## Adoption boundary

**Adopt a ToT pilot only when all are true:**

- The task has costly failures and direct/CoT has a demonstrated quality shortfall.
- It can be decomposed into small, valid intermediate states.
- There is a reliable state or final-output evaluator, preferably external or deterministic rather than solely LM self-evaluation.
- The workflow can tolerate the measured-order cost increase and multi-call latency.
- A bounded search policy, stop rule, and fallback are specified.

Otherwise, retain direct or CoT prompting. For open-ended writing-like tasks, include iterative refinement as a first-class comparator because it outscored standalone ToT in the original result.

## Proposed control: paired local evaluation

This is a recommendation, not a result from the paper.

1. **Dataset:** Freeze a representative, held-out local set. Stratify by difficulty and failure consequence. Define acceptance criteria before runs.
2. **Matched conditions:** Hold model/version, temperature, prompt examples, output budget, tool access, and retry policy constant. Compare direct IO, CoT, and ToT. Include iterative refinement where the task is open-ended.
3. **ToT variants:** Pre-register decomposition, candidate count, breadth, evaluation method, vote count, maximum nodes, early-stop rule, and fallback to CoT.
4. **Measurement:** Per case record final quality, verifier outcome, human-blinded rating where needed, input/output tokens, calls, dollar cost, median and p95 latency, evaluator disagreement, pruning errors, and failure modes.
5. **Decision rule:** Adopt only if ToT’s incremental quality over the best matched non-ToT baseline exceeds a predeclared threshold **and** remains worthwhile at p95 cost/latency. Report quality and cost jointly, not a best-of-\(k\) oracle comparison.

## Remaining gaps and stop condition

The original evidence does not provide a current-model replication, production latency distribution, a universally reliable evaluator, or a cost-normalized local workflow result. It also does not define the Table 7/8 slash token labels beyond “Generate/Prompt” while prose calls the first value completion tokens. Research stops here because the controlled phase permits only the two frozen original-source files.
