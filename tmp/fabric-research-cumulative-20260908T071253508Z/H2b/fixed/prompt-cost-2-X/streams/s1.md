# Tree of Thoughts: original-evidence decision note

## Assigned question and scope

Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct input-output (IO) or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, based only on the original paper and its linked appendix formats?

**Bounded answer:** **No blanket adoption.** The paper supports a conditional trial of ToT for tasks where direct/CoT materially fail and where the workflow can define compact intermediate states, generate alternatives, and evaluate/prune them. It does not establish a general production advantage or a favorable cost-quality ratio outside its three purpose-built tasks. [Original paper, §4 and §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2)

## Measured findings

### Game of 24

- **Task and metric:** 100 relatively hard games, indices 901–1,000 from 4nums.com. A result succeeds only if it is a valid equation equaling 24 and uses each input number exactly once. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)
- **Model condition:** Chat Completion GPT-4, temperature 0.7, experiments run May 5–16, 2023 unless stated otherwise. [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4)
- **Direct and CoT comparators:**  
  - IO: five in-context examples, 100 samples per game.  
  - CoT: same structure augmented with three intermediate equations, 100 samples per game.  
  - CoT self-consistency: majority final output from 100 CoT samples.  
  - Iterative refinement: up to 10 refinements from an IO sample, explicitly using ground-truth equation-correctness feedback. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)
- **Reported average-sampling results:** IO **7.3%**, CoT **4.0%**, CoT self-consistency **9.0%** success. ToT with breadth \(b=1\) reached **45%**, and BFS ToT with \(b=5\) reached **74%**. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)
- **Stronger sampling comparator:** the paper’s *oracle* “best of 100” CoT reached **49%**, versus ToT’s **74%**. This is not ordinary deployable CoT unless an external verifier can select the correct sample. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1); [Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T7)
- **ToT condition:** three equation thoughts; sequential proposal prompt; BFS retaining \(b=5\) states per step; candidate valuation as “sure/maybe/impossible,” sampled three times per thought. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

### Creative Writing

- **Task and metric:** 100 inputs, each four random sentences. Output must be a coherent four-paragraph passage ending paragraphs with those sentences. No ground-truth passage exists. Quality was GPT-4’s zero-shot 1–10 coherence score, five scores averaged per output, plus a blind author-subset comparison of CoT versus ToT. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)
- **Comparators:** zero-shot IO direct generation and zero-shot CoT “brief plan then write,” with 10 samples per task. Iterative refinement used up to five refinements from a random IO output. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)
- **ToT condition:** depth two, \(k=5\) plans then five votes to select one, followed by \(k=5\) passages conditioned on that plan and five votes. Only one choice was retained at each step, \(b=1\). [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)
- **Reported quality:** GPT-4 mean coherence: IO **6.19**, CoT **6.93**, ToT **7.56**. Humans preferred ToT in **41/100** pairs, CoT in **21/100**, and judged **38/100** similarly coherent. Iterative refinement reached **7.67** from IO and **7.91** from ToT. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)
- **Qualification:** the authors explicitly call the automatic GPT-4 metric potentially noisy. The human comparison is only ToT versus CoT, uses an author subset, and leaves 38% tied. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

**Measured cost implications:**

- Game of 24 ToT cost **$0.74/case**, 1.6× the paper’s best-of-100 CoT cost and 5.7× best-of-100 IO, while exceeding their oracle-selected success by 25 and 41 percentage points, respectively.
- Creative ToT cost **about 5×** IO/CoT: $0.32 versus $0.06/$0.07, with 4k versus 0.9k generated tokens. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)
- The two 100-case main ToT experiments cost about **$106**: \(100×\$0.74 + 100×\$0.32\). Crosswords DFS was stated only as “within $100.” [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)
- The authors’ broad range is **5–100× more generated tokens than CoT**, depending on prompts and search algorithm. It is not a fixed ToT multiplier. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

## Transfer limits and counterevidence

1. **Narrow task evidence.** The paper created three relatively simple tasks intended to challenge GPT-4. It says ToT may be unnecessary on tasks GPT-4 already handles well. [§6; Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6)
2. **Self-evaluation is a failure point.** The crossword analysis reports that a correct state could be pruned as “impossible”; removing pruning found correct solutions that the pruning setup could not solve within 100 steps, but the output heuristic surfaced only one. [§4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS3)
3. **Model dependence.** GPT-3.5 ToT was **19%** on Game of 24 versus GPT-4 ToT’s **74%**. GPT-4 generation plus GPT-3.5 evaluation reached **64%**, whereas GPT-3.5 generation plus GPT-4 evaluation reached **31%**, suggesting generation quality was the bottleneck there. [Appendix B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2)
4. **Selection and evaluation differ by task.** Game-of-24 “best of 100” is an oracle comparison. Creative Writing uses model judging and a limited human comparison, not an externally verifiable correctness metric.
5. **No latency or production reliability evidence.** The original cost tables quantify tokens and API dollars, but not end-to-end latency, tail latency, retry behavior, tool integration, or current-model economics.
6. **Source-format defect:** the primary HTML caption for Table 8 says “Cost analysis on Game of 24,” while its header and rows clearly say Creative Writing. This is a source caption/header mismatch, not a corrected value. [Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T8)

## Proposed adoption boundary

**Proposed control, not measured paper evidence:** adopt ToT only behind a routing gate for cases that meet all of these conditions:

- Baseline direct and CoT performance is materially below the workflow’s required quality.
- The task has small, explicit, valid intermediate states and a meaningful branch/prune or verifier signal.
- A wrong early step can be recovered through search or backtracking.
- The added token, dollar, and latency budgets are acceptable for the specific case.
- Direct/CoT remains the default for easy, latency-sensitive, or weakly verifiable tasks.

## Proposed matched local evaluation

**Proposed control, not measured paper evidence:**

1. Build a frozen, representative evaluation set split by task type and difficulty. Include externally checked cases where possible and independently blinded human judgments for open-ended outputs.
2. Run IO, CoT, and ToT on the **same model version, system prompt, context, temperature, tool access, and test cases**. Pre-register output limits, retries, and a per-case cost/latency cap.
3. Compare a normal deployable setting first: one IO, one CoT, and bounded ToT. If sampling is allowed, give every arm the same total token or dollar budget. Report a separate oracle/verifier-selected analysis only where a production-equivalent verifier exists.
4. For ToT, sweep a small fixed grid of branch width, vote count, and depth. Log generated/prompt tokens separately, calls, wall time, branching, pruning decisions, verifier outcomes, and final failure mode.
5. Decide per task stratum: promote ToT only if its quality lift exceeds a pre-set practical threshold **and** its cost, p95 latency, and error profile fit the service budget. Retain the cheapest passing method otherwise.
