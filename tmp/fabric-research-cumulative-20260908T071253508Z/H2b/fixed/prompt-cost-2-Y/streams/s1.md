# Tree of Thoughts: original evidence on quality, cost, and adoption

## Assigned question and scope

**Question:** Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct/input-output (IO) or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, based on the original study’s measured quality/cost evidence?

**Bounded answer:** **Do not replace direct or CoT prompting wholesale.** The original paper supports a conditional ToT option for tasks that are both difficult for the tested model under CoT and have a useful, evaluable intermediate-state search structure. It does not establish a general production advantage, a result on current models, or an advantage on routine/easy or knowledge-bound tasks.

This note uses only the frozen original-paper extracts and raw primary table recovery specified in the assignment. “Direct” means the paper’s **IO prompting**.

## Measured findings

### F1. Game of 24: substantial quality gain, but against several materially different comparators

On 100 relatively hard Game of 24 instances, indexed 901–1,000 from 4nums.com, success required a valid equation equal to 24 using every input number exactly once. The paper used GPT-4 Chat Completion, temperature 0.7, with experiments conducted May 5–16, 2023. [Methods and results (§4.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

| Condition | Measured success | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| IO, 5-shot, 100 samples per game, averaged | 7.3% | Standard direct-prompt baseline |
| CoT, 3 intermediate equations, 100 samples per game, averaged | 4.0% | Single-chain CoT was worse than IO here |
| CoT self-consistency, majority answer from 100 CoT samples | 9.0% | Majority vote, not oracle selection |
| ToT BFS, breadth \(b=1\) | 45% | Search/evaluation even at one retained state |
| ToT BFS, breadth \(b=5\) | 74% | Main ToT result |
| IO “best of 100” oracle | 33% | Retrospective best-of-samples comparison, not a deployable selector as described |
| CoT “best of 100” oracle | 49% | Same oracle framing |
| Iterative refine over IO, up to 10 iterations | Result cell unavailable in authorized text | It used ground-truth equation-correctness feedback, so it is not a like-for-like no-external-feedback comparator |

[Cost Table 7 / Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3) reports **74% ToT versus 49% CoT best-of-100**, which is the paper’s key matched-compute directional contrast. The abstract’s “4% vs. 74%” contrast is single-chain CoT versus ToT, not CoT with 100 independent samples.

**ToT mechanism and conditions:** Three thought steps, each an intermediate equation. At each step, the model proposed next steps and BFS retained the best \(b=5\) candidates. A GPT-4 evaluator classified candidates as “sure/maybe/impossible” with respect to reaching 24; values were sampled **three times per thought**. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

**Mechanism evidence:** The paper reports about 60% of CoT samples failed after their first step. This is consistent with search helping where an early locally plausible move destroys solvability. It is not evidence that search helps when a task lacks that property. [§4.1 error analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

### F2. Creative Writing: a smaller, evaluator-dependent quality gain at about 5× cost

The task gave four random sentences and required a coherent four-paragraph passage ending each paragraph with one supplied sentence. There was no reference passage. Across 100 inputs, the paper reported: [Methods and results (§4.2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

| Condition | Measured outcome |
|---|---:|
| IO, zero-shot | GPT-4 coherence score 6.19 / 10 |
| CoT, zero-shot plan then write | 6.93 / 10 |
| ToT | 7.56 / 10 |
| Iterative refine from IO | 7.67 / 10 |
| Iterative refine from ToT | 7.91 / 10 |
| Blind human ToT vs. CoT comparison | ToT preferred 41/100, CoT 21/100, tie 38/100 |

The automatic score was five GPT-4 zero-shot scores per output, averaged. The paper reports mean within-output standard deviation of about 0.56. Human evaluation used a subset of the authors, blinded to condition and with randomized output order. These conditions make the human result useful but not an independent production-quality measurement. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

**ToT mechanism and conditions:** depth 2 with one plan step. Generate five plans, sample five votes to select one, generate five passages from that plan, then sample five votes to select one. Breadth was \(b=1\), so this is staged sample-and-vote selection rather than retained multi-branch BFS. IO and CoT each generated 10 samples per task. The authorized source text does not state how those 10 baseline samples were selected or aggregated for the reported score, so that selection detail is unavailable. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

### F3. Original token and cost accounting

The primary Appendix B.3 table header is **“Generate/Prompt tokens.”** The accompanying prose calls the first number “completion tokens,” but does not define the slash convention further. Values below preserve the published labels. [Appendix B.3 and Tables 7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

| Task / condition | Generate/Prompt tokens per case | Cost per case | Quality field |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Game of 24 IO, best of 100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| Game of 24 CoT, best of 100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| Game of 24 ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |
| Creative Writing IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 | Not reported in table |
| Creative Writing CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 | Not reported in table |
| Creative Writing ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 | Not reported in table |

The paper therefore shows an important operational distinction:

- **Game of 24:** ToT used fewer generated tokens than 100-sample CoT (5.5k vs. 6.7k), yet had a higher reported success rate, but cost more per case ($0.74 vs. $0.47). Prompt-token accounting, repeated search calls, and then-current price structure matter.
- **Creative Writing:** ToT used about 4.4× generated tokens relative to IO/CoT (4.0k vs. 0.9k), about 7.25× prompt tokens relative to IO/CoT (2.9k vs. 0.4k), and about 5× the money cost ($0.32 vs. $0.06/$0.07).

The paper states the two main ToT experiment runs cost approximately **$106**: \(100 \times \$0.74 + 100 \times \$0.32\). It says Crossword DFS experiments “should be also within $100,” not a measured exact total. It generalizes only that ToT could require **5–100× more generated tokens than CoT**, depending on prompts and search algorithm. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

**Source-format qualification:** The published HTML caption for Table 8 incorrectly says “Cost analysis on Game of 24,” while the table header and rows clearly identify Creative Writing. This note preserves that discrepancy rather than silently correcting the caption.

## Limitations and counterevidence

### F4. Transfer boundary is narrow

The authors explicitly limit the work to three relatively simple tasks chosen because they challenge GPT-4. They state deliberate search may not be necessary for many tasks GPT-4 already performs well on. [Discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6)

Their own Appendix says zero-shot ToT improved GPT-4 over CoT only **slightly** on GSM8K and StrategyQA because GPT-4+CoT was already strong, and because StrategyQA’s bottleneck was external knowledge rather than reasoning. Their recommendation is smaller LLM + ToT for traditional NLP tasks, or GPT-4 + ToT for hard tasks that challenge GPT-4 + CoT. [Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS1)

### F5. Search quality depends on an imperfect model-based evaluator

The demonstrated mechanism requires a thought decomposition that is small enough to generate diverse candidates but large enough for the model to evaluate. It also depends on a useful evaluator and search algorithm. [Framework (§3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S3)

The paper’s Crossword ablations show the risk: the evaluator could prune a correct state, and removing pruning found correct solutions in some cases that pruned ToT did not solve within the 100-step limit. This is direct counterevidence to treating self-evaluation as reliably correct. [§4.3 ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS3)

### F6. The quality measurements do not establish present-day production economics

All main results use the then-tested GPT-4 Chat Completion model and temperature. Costs are therefore historical measurements of a specific API/model/prompt/search configuration, not a forecast for a current production model. The paper does not provide p50/p95 latency, rate-limit behavior, failure-recovery cost, or end-to-end operational reliability.

Creative Writing has no ground truth and relies on GPT-4-as-judge plus a small author subset for human comparisons. Game of 24 is a narrow, fully verifiable arithmetic-search task. Neither result establishes correctness, safety, or cost-effectiveness for a production domain.

## Adoption boundary

**Measured conclusion:** Adopt ToT only as an evaluated optional workflow, not as the default replacement for IO or CoT.

**Proposed control, not a paper result:** Permit a ToT candidate only for a task class with all of the following:

1. CoT has a demonstrated local failure mode on the target cases, especially an early irreversible decision.
2. The task admits meaningful intermediate states, multiple alternatives, and a valid selection or verification signal.
3. The extra token, API, latency, and implementation budget is justified by the incremental quality value.
4. The system can safely handle evaluator error, including retaining alternatives or using an external deterministic validator when available.

Keep IO/CoT as default for routine cases, tasks already solved adequately by CoT, and knowledge-bound cases where branching cannot supply missing information. The latter is an applicability boundary from the paper, not a claim that ToT was negatively tested on every such task.

## Proposed paired local evaluation

This is a proposed decision test, separate from the measurements above.

### Design

1. **Freeze the production target.** Use the same current model/version, temperature, system prompt, task context, tools, timeout, and output validator for every arm.
2. **Create two pre-labeled cohorts.**
   - **Mechanism cohort:** representative hard cases with verifiable multi-step states, early-decision sensitivity, and a legitimate backtracking or candidate-selection opportunity.
   - **Exploratory boundary cohort:** routine/easy cases and knowledge-limited cases. Do not use this cohort as a source-established ToT gate. Use it to detect transfer failure and unnecessary spend.
3. **Run paired conditions on every case.**
   - Direct IO.
   - CoT.
   - CoT multi-sample/self-consistency with a deployable selection rule.
   - ToT with explicit thought unit, candidate count, evaluator, breadth/depth, pruning policy, stop rule, and fallback.
4. **Match resources in two analyses.**
   - **Budget-matched:** cap total generated tokens, prompt tokens, requests, and wall-clock latency equally across CoT multi-sample and ToT.
   - **Service-level-matched:** compare each arm at the production latency and dollar ceiling, including all repeated prompts, votes, retries, and discarded branches.
5. **Measure:** task success via an independent deterministic validator or blinded domain judgment, quality severity, abstention/fallback rate, evaluator-selection error, total prompt and generated tokens, API cost, request count, p50/p95 latency, and variance across repeated runs.
6. **Pre-register the adoption rule:** ToT must improve the primary quality metric on the mechanism cohort while meeting the selected cost and latency ceiling and without a material rise in severe failures. Report exploratory-cohort outcomes separately.
