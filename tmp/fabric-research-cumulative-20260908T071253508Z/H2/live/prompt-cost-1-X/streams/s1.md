# Tree of Thoughts adoption evidence note

## Assigned question and bounded answer

**Question.** Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct input-output (IO) or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, based on the original study’s quality, compute, and cost evidence?

**Bounded answer.** **Do not adopt ToT as the default production workflow from this paper alone.** The original evidence supports a conditional use case: expensive, bounded-depth problems where (a) direct/CoT performance is demonstrably inadequate, (b) intermediate states can be generated and meaningfully ranked, and (c) extra inference cost and latency are acceptable. It does not establish transfer to ordinary production tasks, current models, or a general superiority ranking.

**Scope.** Only Yao et al., arXiv v2 / NeurIPS 2023, its PDF/HTML appendix, and the paper-linked official repository, retrieved live. The paper’s main GPT-4 experiments ran **May 5–16, 2023**. [Paper §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4)

## Measured evidence

### Game of 24: large gain, deliberately hard and highly structured

The test set was 100 relatively hard puzzles, indices 901–1,000 from 4nums.com. Success required a valid equation equaling 24 and using every input number exactly once. [§4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

| Method | Success | Comparator condition |
|---|---:|---|
| IO prompt | 7.3% | 5 in-context examples, 100 samples/game averaged |
| CoT prompt | 4.0% | 3 explicit intermediate equations, 100 samples/game averaged |
| CoT self-consistency | 9.0% | Majority answer from 100 CoT samples |
| IO + refine | 27% | Up to 10 iterations, with ground-truth equation-correctness feedback |
| IO best of 100 | 33% | Oracle selection using known correctness |
| CoT best of 100 | 49% | Oracle selection using known correctness |
| ToT, breadth 1 | 45% | Three equation-thought steps |
| ToT, breadth 5 | 74% | BFS, retain five states each step |

[Original Table 2 and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

**ToT condition.** GPT-4 Chat Completions, temperature 0.7 unless stated otherwise. It used a one-example sequential “propose” prompt, three equation steps, BFS with `b=5`, and three LM value samples per state, classifying reachability as `sure/maybe/impossible`. [§4 and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4)

**Interpretation.** The meaningful measured contrast is **74% ToT versus 4% sampled CoT**, and also **74% versus 49% oracle best-of-100 CoT**. This supports structured search plus LM evaluation on this task, not merely more independent samples. But “best of 100” is not a deployable baseline without an external verifier or equivalent selector. The refine baseline is also advantaged by ground-truth feedback unavailable in many production tasks.

### Creative Writing: smaller, subjective quality evidence and a counter-result

The invented task required four paragraphs ending in four supplied random sentences, over 100 inputs. There was no reference output. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

| Method | Mean GPT-4 coherence score, 1–10 |
|---|---:|
| IO | 6.19 |
| CoT | 6.93 |
| ToT | 7.56 |
| IO + refine | 7.67 |
| ToT + refine | 7.91 |

[§4.2 and Figure 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

The automatic evaluation used five GPT-4 scores per output, averaged. The reported average within-output standard deviation was about **0.56**. In a blind comparison by a subset of the authors, ToT was preferred to CoT in **41/100** pairs, CoT in **21/100**, and **38/100** were similarly coherent. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

**ToT condition.** Depth two: generate five plans, take five votes to select one, generate five passages from that plan, then take five votes to select one. Only one state was retained after each stage (`b=1`). IO and CoT each generated ten samples/task. The linked experiment scripts use GPT-4 at temperature **1.0** for the creative-writing runs. [Official scripts](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm/tree/master/scripts/text)

**Counterevidence.** Iterative refinement outperformed plain ToT on the paper’s own automatic coherence metric: **7.67 vs. 7.56** when refining IO. Therefore, this experiment does not support “use ToT rather than all simpler workflows.” It supports a quality gain over the paper’s plain IO/CoT prompts, with a subjective, model-mediated metric and limited author human evaluation.

## Original cost and token accounting

The appendix reports **per-case generation/completion tokens / prompt tokens** and dollar cost. It does not provide latency, token pricing, variance, or a full all-baseline experiment bill.

| Task and method | Generate / prompt tokens per case | Cost per case | Quality |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Game24, IO best of 100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| Game24, CoT best of 100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| Game24, ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |
| Creative Writing, IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 | 6.19 |
| Creative Writing, CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 | 6.93 |
| Creative Writing, ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 | 7.56 |

[Appendix B.3, Tables 7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

**Measured cost implications.**

- Game24 ToT generated fewer completion tokens than 100 CoT trials, **5.5k vs. 6.7k**, yet cost more, **$0.74 vs. $0.47/case**. Its prompt-token count was lower too, **1.4k vs. 2.2k**. The paper does not explain the apparent dollar/token relationship, so it should not be reconstructed as a general pricing claim.
- Creative-writing ToT used about **4.4×** the completion tokens and **4.6×** the stated money cost versus CoT, and about **7.25×** prompt tokens.
- The reported cost to complete the two **main ToT** experiments was approximately **$106**: `0.74 × 100 + 0.32 × 100`. Crosswords DFS was estimated as within $100. This is not a total study cost and not a current cost forecast.
- The authors generalize that ToT can require **5–100×** more generated tokens than CoT, depending on prompts and search algorithm. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

**Source qualification.** Table 8 is captioned “Cost analysis on Game of 24” although its contents and adjacent discussion are Creative Writing. The values above follow the table body and surrounding text, preserving that source inconsistency.

## Transfer evidence and limitations

### Measured transfer within the paper

- On 100 random GSM8K test questions, zero-shot GPT-4 IO/CoT/ToT scored **51/86/90**. On 100 random StrategyQA development questions, they scored **73/82/83**. ToT sampled five strategies, voted, sampled five solutions, and voted. [Appendix B.1, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS1)  
  **Implication:** gains were only +4 and +1 points over CoT, respectively. The authors attribute StrategyQA’s bottleneck to external knowledge rather than reasoning.
- GPT-3.5 results were materially weaker. Game24 IO/CoT/ToT was **6%/3%/19%**, versus GPT-4’s **7.3%/4.0%/74%**. The GPT-3.5 ToT Game24 prompt was changed from one-shot to three-shot. [Appendix B.2, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2)
- Creative-writing GPT-3.5 IO/CoT/ToT coherence was **4.47/5.16/6.62**, compared with GPT-4’s **6.19/6.93/7.56**. [Appendix B.2, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2)
- In Game24 hybrid ablations, GPT-4 generation plus GPT-3.5 evaluation achieved **64%**, while GPT-3.5 generation plus GPT-4 evaluation achieved **31%**. The authors infer generation was the bottleneck there. [Appendix B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2)

### Strongest limitations

1. **Narrow task construction.** The central three tasks were invented or selected specifically to challenge 2023 GPT-4, and only three relatively simple tasks were studied. The authors explicitly say ToT may not be needed where GPT-4 already excels. [Discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6)
2. **Task-specific search design.** Game24 relies on arithmetic states with an objective verifier. Creative Writing uses bespoke plan/passage/vote prompts and subjective coherence. Production tasks may lack a compact thought unit, reliable evaluator, bounded tree depth, or reversible state.
3. **Selection/evaluation risk.** ToT relies on the same LM to generate and value states. Creative-writing quality was primarily judged by GPT-4, with a small author-only blind study. No independent evaluator, calibration analysis, or confidence intervals are reported.
4. **Comparator asymmetry.** Game24 best-of-100 figures are oracle selections based on known success. Refinement receives explicit correctness feedback. Those results cannot be treated as standard deployable prompt baselines.
5. **Sampling instability.** The official repository says its reproduced `b=5` Game24 run scored **69% rather than the paper’s 74%** due to GPT decoding randomness. [Official repository](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm)
6. **Operationally incomplete evidence.** No end-to-end latency, parallelism behavior, tail cost, token variance, failure recovery, current-model replication, or production-risk evaluation is reported.

## Adoption boundary

**Supported inference.** Enable ToT only behind a routing rule for tasks with all of the following:

- Direct and CoT baselines miss a defined quality threshold.
- The task has a bounded, composable intermediate state and a credible state-ranking or external-verification signal.
- Search can be capped by token, candidate, depth, and wall-clock budgets.
- The expected quality lift is worth materially higher cost and latency.

**Not supported by this evidence.** Replacing direct or CoT prompting globally, forecasting current cost from 2023 dollar figures, or assuming self-voting transfers to unconstrained production reasoning.

## Proposed control: matched local evaluation

This section is a **recommendation**, not a paper result.

1. **Dataset and split.** Assemble 100–300 representative, frozen production-like cases. Stratify by difficulty and failure consequence. Define an objective scorer where possible. For subjective outputs, use blinded independent raters and a preregistered rubric.
2. **Paired treatments.** Run each same case with:
   - direct IO,
   - CoT,
   - CoT self-consistency at a fixed completion-token budget,
   - ToT with task-specific decomposition and evaluator,
   - optionally refine, if it is a realistic production alternative.
3. **Fairness controls.** Pin model/version, system prompt, tool access, temperature, max tokens, retries, and evaluator. Compare both:
   - **equal budget**: same maximum total input plus output tokens or dollars, and
   - **quality-target budget**: lowest cost that reaches a predefined score.
4. **ToT sweeps.** Predefine small search settings, for example breadth 1/3/5 and evaluator votes 1/3/5. Log every branch, pruning decision, generation/evaluation token count, latency, external calls, and final result.
5. **Decision metrics.** Report paired task success or blinded preference, valid-output rate, p50/p95 latency, input/output tokens, cost, evaluator agreement, and failure modes. Bootstrap paired confidence intervals. Separate search quality from selector quality by testing an external or deterministic verifier where available.
6. **Gate.** Adopt only if ToT clears a predeclared material lift while meeting p95 latency and unit-cost limits. Otherwise retain the simpler winner and route only empirically identified hard cases to ToT.

## Remaining gaps and stopping point

The original paper does not supply production-task replication, current-model evidence, latency distributions, or enough pricing detail to convert its dollar costs into current forecasts. It also does not establish a fair non-oracle 100-sample selection baseline for Game24. Research stopped because the allowed original paper, PDF appendix, and linked official repository repeated the same evidence and do not answer those gaps.
