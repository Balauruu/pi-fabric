# Tree of Thoughts: original evidence on quality, cost, and adoption boundary

## Assigned question and scope

**Question:** Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) rather than direct or chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, based on the original study’s measured quality/cost evidence?

**Bounded answer:** **No as a default replacement.** The original study supports ToT as a selective, higher-compute search procedure for tasks deliberately constructed to require planning, branching, and recoverable intermediate states. It does not establish a general production advantage over direct prompting or CoT. Adopt it only after a matched local evaluation shows that its error reduction is worth its materially higher token, latency, and orchestration cost.

Scope is limited to arXiv v2, dated 3 December 2023, its appendix, and the paper-linked original repository and prompts, retrieved live. The reported GPT-4 experiments ran 5–16 May 2023 at temperature 0.7. [Paper §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4)

## Measured findings

### F1. Game of 24: large result under a specialized, search-friendly setup

On 100 relatively hard puzzles, indices 901–1000 from 4nums.com, success required a valid expression equaling 24 and using each input number exactly once. [Paper §4.1, Task Setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

| Condition | Measured success |
|---|---:|
| Direct IO, 5-shot, average over 100 samples per game | 7.3% |
| CoT, 5-shot with three intermediate equations, average over 100 samples | 4.0% |
| CoT self-consistency, majority answer from 100 CoT samples | 9.0% |
| IO + iterative refinement, at most 10 iterations | 27% |
| ToT BFS, breadth \(b=1\) | 45% |
| ToT BFS, breadth \(b=5\) | **74%** |
| IO “best of 100” | 33% |
| CoT “best of 100” | 49% |

Source: [Paper Table 2 and §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.F3).

The relevant operational contrast is **74% ToT versus 49% CoT pass@100-style oracle success**, a 25-point difference. The headline **74% versus 4%** instead compares ToT with a single CoT sample, not compute-matched CoT.

**Conditions matter.** ToT decomposed the task into three arithmetic equations, used a sequential proposal prompt, retained five states at each BFS level, and queried GPT-4 three times per candidate for a `sure/maybe/impossible` value. [Paper §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1) The linked implementation confirms greedy selection over language-model-generated values and caching of repeated value prompts. [BFS implementation](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm/blob/master/src/tot/methods/bfs.py) The task also has an exact external verifier.

**Qualification:** “best of 100” is an oracle upper-bound comparator: it credits a run if *any* of 100 samples is correct. It is not a deployable selection procedure without a verifier. Conversely, the refinement comparator received ground-truth equation-correctness feedback, which ordinary open-ended production tasks may not have. [Paper §4.1 baselines](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

### F2. Creative Writing: a smaller gain, with iterative refinement competitive or better

The paper constructed 100 inputs from four random sentences. The output had to be a coherent four-paragraph passage ending each paragraph with its assigned sentence. There was no reference passage. GPT-4 scored coherence from 1–10, and a subset of the authors conducted a blinded CoT-vs-ToT pairwise comparison. [Paper §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

| Condition | Average GPT-4 coherence score |
|---|---:|
| Direct IO | 6.19 |
| CoT | 6.93 |
| ToT | **7.56** |
| IO + iterative refinement | **7.67** |
| ToT + iterative refinement | 7.91 |

ToT exceeded CoT by **0.63/10**. In the paired human comparison, ToT was preferred in 41/100 pairs, CoT in 21/100, and 38 were judged similarly coherent. [Paper §4.2 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

The writing ToT was shallow rather than a broad recursive tree: depth two, generate five plans and vote five times for one plan, then generate five passages conditioned on that plan and vote five times for one passage. It retained only one choice at each stage. [Paper Figure 4 and §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.F4) Direct and CoT baselines were zero-shot, with ten samples per task. [Paper §4.2 baselines](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

**Counterevidence within the paper:** iterative refinement raised IO from 6.19 to 7.67, above unreﬁned ToT’s 7.56. Therefore this experiment does not support “tree search is the best production upgrade” for open-ended writing. It supports that selected plans helped under this particular generation-and-self-judging protocol.

### F3. Cost and token evidence

The appendix reports these **per-case** totals, in `completion/generation tokens` / `prompt tokens`, with then-current GPT-4-priced cost. [Paper Appendix B.3, Tables 7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

| Task and condition | Generation / prompt tokens | Reported cost per case | Outcome reported |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Game of 24, IO best of 100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| Game of 24, CoT best of 100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| Game of 24, ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |
| Creative Writing, IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 | — |
| Creative Writing, CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 | — |
| Creative Writing, ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 | 7.56 coherence |

The paper says the main 100-case ToT experiments cost approximately **$106**: \(100×\$0.74 + 100×\$0.32\). It estimates the crossword DFS experiments at within another $100. It also states that ToT may require **5–100× more generated tokens than CoT**, depending on prompts and search. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

The linked implementation accumulates API `completion_tokens` and `prompt_tokens` from every call and prices its GPT-4 branch at $0.06/1k completion tokens plus $0.03/1k prompt tokens. [Original linked model wrapper](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm/blob/master/src/tot/models.py)

**F3a. Internal accounting inconsistency:** Applying that stated pricing to the Game of 24 ToT token row gives \(5.5×0.06 + 1.4×0.03 = \$0.372\), not the table’s $0.74. The other listed rows approximately reconcile. The paper does not explain the mismatch. Use $0.74 as the study’s reported total, but do not use the Game of 24 token row to derive an independent cost estimate or a precise cost ratio.

**F3b. Not all comparisons are cost-matched.** The cost table prices Game of 24 against oracle “best of 100” IO/CoT, not the ordinary one-sample IO and CoT rows. It provides no direct per-case cost for those 7.3% and 4.0% conditions. A production decision therefore cannot infer a clean cost-effectiveness frontier from this table alone.

## Transfer limits and counterevidence

- **L1 — Narrow task distribution:** the main evidence is 100 hard arithmetic puzzles with an exact verifier and 100 invented writing constraints with no reference output. It does not measure coding, analysis, operations, safety-critical decisions, real customer traffic, tail latency, or service reliability. [Discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6)
- **L2 — Model- and time-specific:** all main results use May-2023 chat-mode GPT-4. In the paper’s GPT-3.5 extension, Creative Writing scores fell from GPT-4’s IO/CoT/ToT 6.19/6.93/7.56 to GPT-3.5’s 4.47/5.16/6.62. [Appendix B.2, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2) This is evidence that the approach depends on the model’s ability to generate and assess intermediate states.
- **L3 — Self-evaluation can be wrong:** search quality depends on the LM’s value or vote heuristic. The paper’s crossword ablations show pruning and final-state selection can discard correct states, and an oracle best-state selection solves more cases. [Paper §4.3 ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS3)
- **L4 — Evaluation dependence in Creative Writing:** GPT-4 both generated/voted over candidate material and supplied the automatic coherence metric. The human comparison was blind but performed by a subset of the authors. Neither provides an independent, production-grounded utility metric. [Paper §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)
- **L5 — Evidence against broad benefit:** on 100-example subsets of GSM8K and StrategyQA, the paper reports only slight zero-shot ToT gains over already-strong GPT-4 CoT and explicitly notes that ToT may be unnecessary where GPT-4 already excels or where external knowledge is the bottleneck. [Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS1)
- **L6 — Prompt/search design is task-specific:** Game of 24 used a constrained proposer and value classifier. Writing used plan sampling and votes. The original prompts make this specialization explicit. [Game prompts](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm/blob/master/src/tot/prompts/game24.py) · [Writing prompts](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm/blob/master/src/tot/prompts/text.py)

## Adoption boundary

**Measured fact:** ToT can substantially improve success on a bounded symbolic task with verifiable intermediate states and a GPT-4 self-evaluator, and modestly improve a synthetic writing coherence metric at roughly five times the reported writing cost.

**Proposed control:** Use ToT only when all of these hold:

1. The workflow has consequential errors that direct/CoT demonstrably miss.
2. Work can be decomposed into meaningful intermediate states, with a credible selector, verifier, or outcome-based judge.
3. Multiple branches and backtracking are useful rather than merely adding paraphrased samples.
4. The latency and token budget can support candidate generation and evaluation.
5. A local evaluation shows incremental value over direct prompting, CoT, and non-tree alternatives such as refinement or verifier-guided resampling.

Otherwise, retain direct prompting or CoT. For creative or editorial work especially, compare refinement before adding tree orchestration.

## Proposed matched local evaluation

This is a proposed control, not a result from the paper.

1. **Dataset:** Pre-register 100–200 representative, held-out production cases, stratified by difficulty, risk, and availability of an external verifier. Preserve inputs, tool access, and output contract across arms.
2. **Arms, run on every case:** direct IO, CoT, iterative refinement, cost-matched CoT/self-consistency, and ToT. Fix the same current model, system prompt, tool permissions, temperature policy, and maximum elapsed time.
3. **ToT curve:** evaluate breadth \(b=1,3,5\), plus a fixed maximum number of generator and evaluator calls. This separates “structured selection” from raw extra sampling.
4. **Fair selection:** use the same deployable selector in all arms. If a deterministic verifier exists, apply it to every candidate in every applicable arm. Do not report oracle pass@N as the production result. If no verifier exists, use blinded domain experts or an evaluator calibrated against human judgments, separate from the generation model where feasible.
5. **Measures per case:** task utility/correctness, constraint and safety failures, retry rate, prompt and completion tokens, request cost, model-call count, p50/p95 end-to-end latency, and evaluator disagreement.
6. **Decision rule:** pre-register the minimum error or utility improvement needed to offset the incremental cost and p95 latency. Require ToT to beat the best non-tree arm under both an equal-spend comparison and the intended production budget. Report paired confidence intervals or bootstrap intervals, not only averages.
7. **Rollout:** if it passes, route only the eligible task class to ToT and retain direct/CoT as fallback. Log selector confidence and downstream outcomes to detect evaluator failure.

## Remaining gaps and stop point

The original materials do not provide confidence intervals, raw per-case token/cost distributions, latency, a stable GPT-4 snapshot identifier, or a reconciled explanation for the Game of 24 ToT cost discrepancy. They also do not establish production transfer beyond the small appendix extensions. Research stopped because the specified original paper, appendix, and linked formats repeat the same evidence and do not resolve those gaps.
