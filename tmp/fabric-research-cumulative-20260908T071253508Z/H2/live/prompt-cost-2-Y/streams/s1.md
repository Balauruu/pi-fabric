# Tree of Thoughts: original-evidence note

## Assigned question and bounded answer

**Question:** Should a production reasoning workflow adopt Tree of Thoughts (ToT) instead of direct/IO or chain-of-thought (CoT), based on the original study’s quality and cost evidence?

**Bounded answer:** **Do not make a general production replacement.** The original paper supports a **conditional, task-specific escalation**: ToT materially improved results on two purpose-built, small GPT-4-era tasks that were designed to defeat direct and CoT prompting, but at substantially greater inference cost and with bespoke decomposition, generation, evaluation, and search designs. It does not establish transfer to a present production workflow, ordinary tasks, a different model, or an equal-cost comparison.

**Scope:** Original paper, arXiv v2, including its original appendix and linked code/prompts/trajectories only. The HTML identifies v2 as 3 December 2023. Main experiments used Chat Completion GPT-4 at temperature 0.7, run 5–16 May 2023. [Paper §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4)

## Measured findings

### F1. Game of 24: large task-specific gain, but not at like-for-like direct/CoT cost

**Task and metric.** The test is 100 relatively hard games, indices 901–1,000 from 4nums’ 1,362 games. Success requires a valid equation equaling 24 and using each input number exactly once. [§4.1, “Task Setup”](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

| Method | Condition | Success |
|---|---|---:|
| IO | 5 in-context examples, 100 samples per game for average performance | 7.3% |
| CoT | IO pairs augmented with three intermediate equations, 100 samples per game | 4.0% |
| CoT self-consistency | majority output from 100 CoT samples | 9.0% |
| ToT-BFS | breadth \(b=1\) | 45% |
| ToT-BFS | breadth \(b=5\) | **74%** |
| IO + iterative refine | at most 10 iterations | 27% |
| IO oracle best-of-100 | selects a successful sample | 33% |
| CoT oracle best-of-100 | selects a successful sample | 49% |

[Original Table 2 and method passage](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.F3)

**ToT condition.** The authors decomposed the task into three intermediate equations, used a one-example “propose” prompt at each step, searched with BFS, retained \(b=5\) candidates per step, and sampled each candidate’s `sure/maybe/impossible` value three times. This is not merely “ask for more reasoning.” It is a tailored generator, evaluator, and search policy. [§4.1, “ToT Setup”](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

**Cost/accounting per case.** The appendix labels its count as **“Generate/Prompt tokens”** and separately reports dollars. It does not specify the then-current GPT-4 tariff or break out individual search calls.

| Method | Generate / Prompt tokens | Cost per case | Success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IO, oracle best-of-100 | 1.8k / 1.0k | $0.13 | 33% |
| CoT, oracle best-of-100 | 6.7k / 2.2k | $0.47 | 49% |
| ToT | 5.5k / 1.4k | $0.74 | 74% |

[Appendix Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T7)

**Interpretation.** ToT used fewer generated and prompt tokens than best-of-100 CoT, yet cost **1.57×** as much per case ($0.74 vs. $0.47), presumably because token category, call structure, and/or pricing varied. The paper does not enable a more precise attribution. The measured quality contrast is 74% versus the best-of-100 CoT oracle’s 49%, but an oracle “best” selector is not a deployable comparator unless the production workflow has an equally reliable verifier. [§4.1 results and Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS1)

### F2. Creative Writing: moderate judged-quality gain, fivefold cost, refinement erodes the case for ToT alone

**Task and metric.** Inputs are four random sentences. Outputs must be coherent four-paragraph passages ending in those sentences. There is no reference passage. Quality was measured by five GPT-4 zero-shot 1–10 scores averaged per output, and a blinded pairwise human comparison by a subset of the authors. The authors report an average within-output score standard deviation of about 0.56. [§4.2, “Task setup”](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

| Method | GPT-4 coherence score | Human blind comparison, ToT vs. CoT |
|---|---:|---:|
| IO | 6.19 | — |
| CoT, brief plan then passage | 6.93 | CoT preferred: 21/100 |
| ToT | **7.56** | ToT preferred: 41/100 |
| — | — | Similar: 38/100 |
| IO + iterative refine | 7.67 | — |
| ToT + iterative refine | 7.91 | — |

[§4.2 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

**Comparator and ToT conditions.** IO and CoT were zero-shot, with ten samples each per task. CoT made a brief plan before writing. ToT used depth two: sample five plans, vote five times for the best, then sample five passages from that plan and vote five times for the best. Breadth was one because the vote retained one choice per stage. The paper does not make selection and token accounting for the ten-sample baseline fully explicit. [§4.2 methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S4.SS2)

| Method | Generate / Prompt tokens | Cost per case |
|---|---:|---:|
| IO | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.06 |
| CoT | 0.9k / 0.4k | $0.07 |
| ToT | 4.0k / 2.9k | $0.32 |

[Appendix Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T8)

**Interpretation.** ToT’s cost was about **5.3× IO** and **4.6× CoT** while its GPT-4-scored coherence rose 1.37 points over IO and 0.63 over CoT. However, IO plus refinement (7.67) exceeded standalone ToT (7.56), while ToT plus refinement was only 0.24 higher. Thus this experiment supports a quality-cost choice among several deliberate-generation strategies, not a conclusion that ToT dominates iterative refinement.

### F3. Reported experiment spend and broad resource claim

For the 100-case Game of 24 and 100-case Creative Writing main ToT experiments, the paper computes **$106**: \(100×$0.74 + 100×$0.32\). It says crossword DFS experiments “should be also within $100.” It also states ToT can require **5–100×** more generated tokens than CoT, depending on prompts and search algorithm. [Appendix B.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS3)

There is a source-format inconsistency worth preserving: the HTML’s Table 8 is headed “Creative Writing,” but its caption says “Cost analysis on Game of 24.” The accompanying prose and table heading establish that it is the Creative Writing cost table. [Original HTML, Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.T8)

## Counterevidence and transfer limits

### F4. The paper itself limits applicability

The authors state that ToT may be unnecessary on tasks GPT-4 already performs well, and characterize the work as only three relatively simple tasks constructed to challenge GPT-4. [Discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#S6)

Their added zero-shot GPT-4 tests show small changes where CoT was already strong: GSM8K 86 to 90 and StrategyQA 82 to 83. The paper attributes StrategyQA’s bottleneck to external knowledge rather than reasoning. [Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS1)

### F5. Model transfer is incomplete and unfavorable in one important direction

For GPT-3.5, Game of 24 scores were IO 6%, CoT 3%, and ToT 19%, versus GPT-4 ToT’s 74%. The authors changed the proposal prompt from one-shot to three-shot to make GPT-3.5 work. A mixed-model ablation gave 64% for GPT-4 generation plus GPT-3.5 evaluation, and 31% for GPT-3.5 generation plus GPT-4 evaluation, suggesting generation was the bottleneck for this task. [Appendix B.2, Tables 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v2#A2.SS2)

That is evidence that ToT’s effect depends materially on base-model generation capability and prompt design. It is not evidence that a cheaper model plus generic ToT will reproduce GPT-4’s result.

### F6. Strongest design limitations

1. **Bespoke task/search coupling:** Game of 24’s arithmetic-state representation and value categories, and Creative Writing’s plan-and-vote hierarchy, were hand-designed. Transfer requires discovering an equally useful thought unit and evaluator, not simply enabling a library feature.
2. **Small, constructed samples:** both focal tasks have 100 generated/test cases, and Game of 24 uses a selected difficult slice. No uncertainty intervals, seeds, or repeated-experiment variance are reported for the principal rates.
3. **Evaluation validity:** Creative Writing’s automatic scorer is GPT-4 judging GPT-4 outputs, with a limited author-subset human study. It has no external ground truth.
4. **Comparator asymmetry:** Game-of-24 best-of-100 is explicitly an oracle. Iterative refinement has ground-truth equation-correctness feedback, which may not exist in production. Creative Writing’s ten-sample baseline selection and its alignment to Table 8 cost accounting are not fully specified.
5. **Operational latency is unmeasured:** the paper reports token/currency cost, not end-to-end latency, p95, rate-limit behavior, error recovery, or reliability of parsing/voting.
6. **Era specificity:** the tested Chat Completion GPT-4 is from May 2023, at temperature 0.7. The study provides no basis for assuming the numerical deltas or dollar costs apply to current models or prices.

## Adoption boundary

**Measured fact:** ToT beat the paper’s direct and CoT conditions on the focal hard tasks, but used materially more resource and required task-specific search machinery.

**Proposed control:** Adopt ToT only as a **budgeted escalation path** for request classes that meet all of the following:

- A direct/CoT workflow fails a defined quality target on representative cases.
- The task admits coherent intermediate states, multiple useful branches, and a credible state or final-output verifier/ranker.
- The expected business value of the measured incremental success exceeds added token, call, latency, and operational-failure cost.
- The workflow can fall back to direct/CoT when the budget, verifier, or search process fails.

Do not use it as the default for already-high-performing, open-ended, low-latency, or poorly verifiable work.

## Proposed matched local evaluation

This is a proposed control, not a result from the paper.

1. **Dataset:** Freeze a representative, stratified holdout of production-like cases before prompt tuning. Label hard/search-like and routine cases separately. Use independent human or deterministic scoring, not the candidate model alone.
2. **Arms:** Run the same current model, system context, retrieval/tools, output schema, temperature, max output tokens, timeout, and final verifier in:
   - direct/IO;
   - single-path CoT;
   - CoT self-consistency at a predeclared call/token budget;
   - iterative refinement where the same verifier is available;
   - ToT with a documented thought representation, candidate count, evaluator/vote count, pruning rule, depth, and hard budget.
3. **Fair budget views:** Report both:
   - **equal-dollar/equal-token** performance, and
   - **quality-target** cost and latency required to reach it.  
   Do not call a best-of-\(k\) result deployable unless its selection rule is available to every arm.
4. **Instrument every case:** input, prompt, completion, cached, and total tokens; API cost; number of calls and branches; wall-clock latency; p50/p95; search stops; verifier/ranker outcomes; parse/tool failures; and fallback use.
5. **Decision rule:** Promote ToT only for strata where its paired quality gain has a confidence interval excluding the practical minimum gain, and its p95 latency and incremental cost remain within a predeclared cap. Retain direct/CoT as the default otherwise.
6. **Ablate before rollout:** vary beam/candidate count, vote count, depth, evaluator model, and early-stop/pruning. This tests whether a much cheaper shallow configuration captures the gain and detects whether the evaluator, not tree search, is doing the work.

## Remaining gaps and stopping point

The original sources do not provide per-call traces, latency, current-model replication, statistical intervals, a fully specified monetary tariff, or a fully unambiguous Creative Writing baseline-selection-to-cost mapping. Those are material gaps for production adoption and cannot be reconstructed from the paper without importing outside evidence. Research stopped because the original HTML, its appendix, and its linked-format references repeated the same evidence and the assigned scope excludes later studies.
